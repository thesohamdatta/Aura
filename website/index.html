The Problem

The smartphone was never designed for AI.

Smartphones fragment attention through screens. Every AI interaction today requires pulling a device from your pocket, unlocking it, and staring into it. The interface competes with the conversation — and the conversation always loses.

The problem isn't the AI. It's the interface. When the device demands your visual attention, it has already failed.

Third Device
"The smartphone was the second device. Aura is the third — worn, not held. Ambient, not demanding. It does not ask for your eyes."

Humans need three device categories. The PC is stationary and task-oriented — a power tool for deep work, rooted to a desk. The smartphone is mobile but screen-bound, an object that turns every idle moment into a demand for attention.

The third device — a wearable — should be ambient, voice-first, and screenless. It should fade into the background until it is needed, answer without interrupting, and remember without requiring you to stop and look.
